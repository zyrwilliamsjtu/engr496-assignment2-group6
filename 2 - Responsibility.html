Professional Ethics for Engineers · Student reference

Who is responsible?
Build your argument.

This program helps you structure and defend your own analysis of responsibility in systems involving people, organizations, and AI. It organizes your evidence and judgments and asks reflective questions.

How to use it

  1. Choose useful sources. Add references and brief notes that will support your analysis.
  2. Define your case on page 2. Choose a fictional starting case, describe the harm, record key facts, and link relevant sources.
  3. Identify stakeholders and duties. Record their roles, connections, knowledge, authority, and contributions.
  4. Compare three models. Apply causal, hierarchy and organization, and forward-looking approaches to the same case.
  5. Visualize responsibility. Select a stakeholder in the map to record your assessment and reasons. Compare causal contribution, legal liability, and duties to act. Investigate a possible gap and write three final answers: causal, legal, and forward-looking responsibility.
  6. Save and export. Save your JSON project before closing, or export your analysis as text to submit it with your source records.

The program works offline. Source reminders use your notes and tags; URLs do not import publications. Use “Reflect on my answer” beside your final answer to receive offline feedback on its reasoning and support. It cannot verify facts, sources, legal accuracy, or the correctness of an ethical conclusion.

Your sources

Add a full reference, a direct link if available, relevant pages, and a short note about its usefulness. Assign a topic so the program can show it at the relevant model.

Your source records are included in the saved project and exported analysis. You may return here to edit them at any time.

03 / Identify stakeholders and duties

Include affected people and organizations, then add relevant technical systems as components of the case. Technical systems can contribute causally without being rights-bearing stakeholders or legal persons.

05 / Investigate a possible gap

Many contributors, uncertainty, or an opaque model do not automatically establish a gap. A defensible conclusion may be that no gap exists.

Foundational reading: Matthias (2004)

Matthias, A. (2004). The responsibility gap: Ascribing responsibility for the actions of learning automata. Ethics and Information Technology, 6(3), 175–183. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10676-004-3422-1

Use this argument about learning systems and the conditions for responsibility alongside Santoni de Sio and Mecacci’s distinctions and Tigard’s counterargument. A learning system does not automatically establish a responsibility gap.

Switch between causal, legal, and forward-looking responsibility. Select a stakeholder to enter or revise your assessment and reasons directly on the map. Lines show your selected assessment, not a calculated share of responsibility. No selection means unassessed.
